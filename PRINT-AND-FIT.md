The kit illustrates dark-blue prints and camera housing, graphite motor bells, and a black Axisflying Argus ECO F405/60A stack on the graphite frame. The hardware in the renders and GLB is a visual approximation. The actual motor and camera colors depend on the hardware you own.

Use **`wire-protectors.3mf`** for the four enclosed motor-wire protectors, two of each hand. See `wire-protectors-preview.png` for the through-channel and its cross-section. The complete **`dark-blue-print-plate.3mf`** contains 16 pieces including the tilt gauge and bolt-free landing shoes. All three plates use millimeters and a dark-blue material swatch. Select 95A TPU and your printer profile. The GLB is a separate assembled preview. `revision-preview.png` shows the four-post shroud, camera bridge, enclosed wire channel and tape surface. `camera-wire-preview.jpg` shows the assembled nose and the harness underneath.

## Print

- Start with **95A TPU**, a 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers, four walls and 20–30% infill. Use your filament’s temperature and speed profile. The replacement STLs and 3MF plates are oriented for printing.
- Print the **wire protectors flat sole down** on a smooth bed face. The sloped interior roof closes across a short span. Disable support inside the tunnel so the wires can pass end to end. Print the **camera cradle bridge down**, with the post bores vertical. Print the **stack shroud on its flat tail end**, in the supplied orientation. Its two window ends taper as the print grows. Use a 5 mm brim on the shroud if your printer needs more bed adhesion. Keep support out of the camera bores and intake passages. Inspect the first layers and clear any stringing before assembly.
- Print the landing shoes tread down. Clear the transverse tie tunnels before assembly. Localized support may help at the angled SMA seat or in the bolt-on bumper pockets.

## Install

1. **Camera cradle.** With the upper carbon plate off, slide both short sleeves over the outer nose posts on the 19 mm pitch. Seat the low bridge on the nose plate, in front of the camera. The rear is open for the plug. Insert the 19 mm Phoenix 2 case from the rear. Start one M2 screw on each side before tightening either. The cheek gap is 19.3 mm and the screw passes through 3.4 mm of TPU at the seat. Choose its length to suit the thread depth in your camera. Loosening the two screws adjusts tilt about their common axis, 19.1 mm behind the nose posts. The top plate captures the raised cheek pads when reinstalled.

   Start at **15° or less** with a wide lens. A 16.8 mm barrel cleared the TPU and stock frame at 0–17° in CAD. It first touched the underside of the stock top plate at 18°. The standard 13.9 mm barrel cleared at 0–20°. The 6.5 mm openings at the tips of the shroud give driver access to these screws. Fit the top plate and verify the lens gap, live view and cable route at your chosen angle.

2. **SMA.** Slide the angled mount onto the outer 22 mm-spaced tail posts. The SMA connector points aft and 30° up. Set the mount’s underside about 14.5 mm above the lower tail plate, and capture it vertically with tubing or split spacers. Check the connector body and flat, washer seating, and thread engagement through the 3.2 mm panel. The outer face accepts a straight SMA antenna. Leave slack in the pigtail and verify clearance to the battery and straps. Check the assembled video link in your usual flight attitude, since signal quality depends on antenna orientation and the receiving setup.
3. **ELRS receiver.** Install the tray facing aft on the inner 20 mm-spaced tail posts. Set its underside about 10.5 mm above the lower tail plate. The illustrated BETAFPV Nano 2.4 GHz receiver has a 12 × 19 mm footprint. Thread the ties through the larger rounded slots before placing the receiver. Check your heat shrink and plug exit, and leave slack at the U.FL connector.
4. **2.4 GHz T antenna.** Slide the saddle onto the outer 22 mm-spaced rear posts beneath the SMA holder, with the saddle facing aft. Set its underside about 3.3 mm above the lower tail plate. Thread a 3.6 mm zip tie through the crosswise tunnel and over the molded antenna hub. Center the horizontal crossbar and route the coax forward with slack at the receiver. The flat seat measures 16.8 × 8.8 mm. CAD checks use a 16 × 8 × 7 mm center housing and an 80 × Ø6 mm crossbar as fit envelopes for a bulkier Thor-style antenna. These are design allowances. Measure your particular antenna before fitting. The tips extend under the rear 5-inch prop disks, so check clearance through a full hand rotation with the battery unplugged and the antenna flexed upward. The assumed lower blade plane is at Z=22 mm. The modeled antenna housing is 4.8 mm below that plane.
5. **Enclosed motor-wire protectors.** Use the right version on the front-right and rear-left arms and the left version on the other diagonal. The 45 mm body follows the arm taper. The wider end faces the stack, and the motor-side end sits **20 mm from the shaft center** along the arm. With both ends of the tunnel clear, feed the three loose motor leads through the motor-side opening toward the ESC before soldering them. The straight bore has a pitched roof, spans about 7.4 mm across, and measures about 4.5 mm from floor to crown. CAD checks used three 2.2 mm insulated wires lying side by side. Dry-fit the wires and leave slack at both ends. Cut **one 6 × 41 mm strip of double-sided 3M tape per arm**, center it on the flat sole, then bond it to clean carbon per the tape instructions. The assembly model uses 0.5 mm tape. Clearances were checked with tape from 0.2 to 1.0 mm.
6. **Bolt-free motor-end bumpers.** The wire holder mounts with tape alone. Each bolt-free shoe still needs its own **3.6 mm zip tie**. With props off, seat the bumper from below, thread its tie through the transverse tunnel and loop it over the shallow band in the wire holder, 34 mm from the motor center. Keep the tie head low on the side. The shoe pocket is 26.6 mm across and about 4.6 mm deep. Check clearance to the installed screw heads and the shaft, then pull and twist the shoe by hand. Check it again after a hard landing.

   For the **bolt-on alternatives**, match the motor’s 16 mm or 19 mm square pattern. These versions add a nominal 3.1 mm under the motor screw heads. Verify thread engagement, motor winding clearance, and shaft-clip clearance.

7. **Front and rear skids.** Seat each skid against the underside of its end plate. It uses the two existing lower screws on the 19 mm front or 22 mm rear posts. The recessed screw seats add about 2.9 mm to the required length. The recesses are 7.2 mm across, with flared entrances. Verify thread engagement and that screws do not bottom in the posts. The skid bottoms sit slightly above the motor-foot landing plane so the four feet take a level landing.
8. **Stack shroud and USB.** Remove the top plate and fit the camera cradle first. Plug in the camera and route its harness down the center between the forward posts. Slide the single shroud over the four inner posts, with the two scoops toward the camera and the straight crossbar aft. Its nose walls slide **outside** the camera cheeks. Match the two round access holes to the camera screws. The front sleeves seat on the lower nose plate and locate the shroud vertically. The short rear mounts sit above the ELRS tray. Guide each motor bundle through an end of the side window, leaving the middle clear for the USB plug. Refit the carbon top plate and check full USB plug seating and clearance to solder joints, wiring and propellers.

   The shroud sides extend about 27 mm beyond the scoop faces. They overlap the camera cheeks by about 11 mm at harness height. Each overlap has about 0.5 mm of hidden fitting clearance. Both sides cover the harness between the lower and upper plates, and the rear of the camera remains accessible from above when the top plate is off. A 4 mm cable bundle and a 4.8 mm driver shank cleared in CAD. Check for a free harness loop and for pinched insulation before fitting the top plate.

   The side openings have unbroken upper and lower rails. In CAD, a 13 × 8 mm plug housing passes through either window with its center up to 5.5 mm fore or aft of the stack center and 6.3–14.0 mm above the top of the central lower carbon plate. A centered plug also cleared at 5.1 mm above that plate. The scoop passages open inward toward the board. Airflow, cooling, adhesive retention and impact strength need physical testing.

## Installed FT800 VTX

The assembly shows the T-Motor FT800 5.8 GHz VTX, rated up to 800 mW, in the rear lower bay beneath the ELRS tray. The approximate 23.5 × 21 mm case envelope, with separate mounting ears, cleared the frame and tray in CAD. The supplied stock frame CAD does not show a full 20 × 20 mm hole pattern in this bay. The model shows the VTX in its existing rear position using an assumed placement, with the mounting hardware omitted because its exact arrangement was unspecified. The short MMCX-to-SMA route to the 30° tail bulkhead is schematic. Use your installed lead with a gentle bend and enough slack at both connectors. [T-Motor FT800 specifications](https://www.t-hobby.com/products/tmotor-ft800-vtx)

## Edit the parts

Keep `arm-outline.scad` beside `sourceone-v5-kit.scad`. Choose a part in OpenSCAD Customizer, adjust its parameters, render with **F6**, and export an STL. The camera width, pivot and post bores, wire length and end flare, and rear hardware mounts are editable.

All 16 individual STLs passed single-solid, closed-mesh checks. The shroud, cradle and two wire protectors were also sectioned at 0.2 mm in print orientation. Transverse sections of each wire protector form a closed ring along the 45 mm body, including the tie saddle and both end faces. Every new section connected to the preceding layer or the bed. Clearance checks used the supplied Source One V5 frame and modeled hardware envelopes. A physical print, tape bond, cable fit and camera sightline still need checking on the assembled quad.
